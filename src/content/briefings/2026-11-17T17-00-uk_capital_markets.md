The defining narrative of today’s session was the palpable tension between speculative exuberance in the artificial intelligence sector and a starkly sobering reality in the UK labour market. The market’s attention was undeniably captured by the impending initial public offering of Anthropic, the developer behind the Claude large language model. However, the frenzy surrounding this event has been amplified to a degree that warrants caution, with the emergence of 3x leveraged exchange-traded funds targeting the company before it has even priced. This is a classic sign of froth; while the underlying technology remains transformative, the financial engineering surrounding the IPO suggests that retail and speculative capital are chasing momentum rather than fundamentals. For institutional clients, this is a reminder that while AI remains the dominant growth vector, the valuation gap between established players and pre-IPO hype is widening dangerously. We must tread with caution, as the market appears to be pricing in perfection for a sector that is still navigating significant regulatory and competitive headwinds.

Contrasting this tech-driven optimism, the macroeconomic backdrop in the UK remains fragile, with the labour market showing signs of structural weakness that could complicate the Bank of England’s path. Job vacancies have plummeted to their lowest level in twelve years, a figure that excludes the pandemic period, as employers grow increasingly reluctant to hire amid soaring employment costs. This data point is critical for our sterling outlook; if the jobs data continues to weaken, it will dampen bets on a Bank of England rate hike, potentially putting downward pressure on the pound against the euro. This divergence is interesting: we have a tech sector that is globally competitive and attracting massive capital, set against a domestic economy that is struggling with high input costs and a cooling labour market. The pound’s trajectory will likely be dictated by this tug-of-war between global tech sentiment and domestic economic resilience.

In the fixed income space, a fascinating behavioural shift is underway among UK retail investors, who are piling into long-dated 30-year gilts. These bonds, which bore the brunt of this year’s selloff, are now attracting buyers looking to lock in yields above 5%. This is a notable departure from the typical retail preference for short-dated, low-risk instruments and suggests a growing appetite for duration risk among non-institutional players. Simultaneously, the asset management landscape is showing signs of renewed confidence in quality. Nick Train’s decision to increase his stake in Finsbury Growth & Income, following record returns, signals a vote of confidence in the trust’s long-awaited recovery. This move by a veteran manager is a strong signal that the market is beginning to reward patience and quality in the UK equity space, even as the broader market remains volatile.

On the corporate and regulatory front, the M&A activity continues to reflect geopolitical complexities. Pressure is mounting on Artem Volynets, a well-connected Russian businessman, regarding his bid for a Norwegian Arctic copper mine. Despite his denials of connections to Russia, the scrutiny highlights the ongoing challenges in cross-border deals involving entities with Russian ties, particularly in critical minerals and defence-adjacent sectors. Meanwhile, the UK government’s stance on digital assets remains supportive, with Boris Johnson reaffirming the UK’s ambition to lead in blockchain at the Solana Breakpoint conference. This policy consistency is crucial for the crypto and digital asset sector, providing a stable regulatory environment that contrasts with the uncertainty in other jurisdictions.

Looking ahead, the key trend to watch is the decoupling of UK domestic economic performance from global tech sentiment. The labour market data will be the primary driver for sterling and gilts, while the Anthropic IPO will serve as a bellwether for the sustainability of the AI trade. Tomorrow, we should watch for any spillover effects from the Anthropic pricing on broader tech indices, as well as any further commentary from the Bank of England on the implications of the cooling labour market for monetary policy. The market is clearly bifurcated, with capital flowing into high-yield, high-growth tech and long-dated bonds, while domestic equities and the broader economy face a more cautious outlook.
