The defining narrative of the session was a decisive decoupling of global risk sentiment from the specific headwinds facing the semiconductor sector. While the broader equity landscape celebrated a relief rally, driven by easing geopolitical tensions in the Middle East and a sharp drop in crude oil prices, the technology complex remained fractured. The FTSE 100 climbed, buoyed by a surge in precious metal miners as gold prices firmed, but this broad-based optimism was tempered by a significant sell-off in Nvidia. The chip giant’s decline, triggered by a fresh Chinese spy scandal, served as a stark reminder that while macro risks are receding, structural and regulatory risks in the AI supply chain remain acute. This divergence suggests that investors are currently prioritizing defensive positioning and commodity exposure over high-beta growth, at least until clarity emerges on the semiconductor front.

In the fixed income and currency markets, the tone was one of cautious optimism, with global government bond markets rallying in October to reverse three consecutive months of sell-offs. This shift in the bond curve has provided a supportive backdrop for equities, particularly as lower oil prices have eased inflationary fears. Sterling reflected this improved risk appetite, reclaiming the 1.3400 level against the US dollar as President Trump downplayed potential escalations in the Iran conflict. The pound’s strength was further supported by its position at the 21-day average, with market participants now looking ahead to Tuesday’s US CPI data and Thursday’s UK GDP figures for the next directional catalyst. Meanwhile, sterling buyers pressed the top of the range against the Canadian dollar, taking advantage of weak Canadian jobs data, indicating a continued preference for the pound in cross-currency pairs.

On the corporate and structural front, the UK market continues to see an evolution in retail access to global capital. Robinhood’s launch of IPO Access in the UK marks a significant milestone, allowing domestic retail investors to request shares in selected US IPOs at the offer price. This move not only deepens the liquidity pool for US-listed tech firms but also signals a growing integration of UK retail capital with US growth narratives. For institutional clients, this highlights a broader trend of borderless capital flows, where UK-based investors are increasingly participating in the primary market of the world’s largest tech ecosystem. It is a subtle but important shift in the landscape of how capital is allocated, potentially increasing volatility in US tech listings as UK demand is factored into the mix.

Looking at the sectoral breakdown, the energy complex saw a pullback as crude prices dropped, alleviating some of the pressure on inflation-sensitive sectors. However, the resilience of gold miners within the FTSE 100 suggests that a portion of the market is still hedging against tail risks, even as the immediate geopolitical threat recedes. The defence and aerospace sectors, which had been elevated by Middle East tensions, likely saw a rotation out of the group, with capital flowing back into cyclical and commodity-linked names. The key takeaway is that the market is currently in a transition phase: moving from a war-risk premium to a data-driven regime, where the focus shifts from geopolitical headlines to fundamental economic indicators.

Tomorrow’s session will be critical in determining whether this relief rally has legs or if it is merely a pause before the next leg of volatility. With US inflation data due on Tuesday and UK GDP on Thursday, the market is poised for a high-impact week. We should watch closely for any divergence between the US and UK economic prints, as this will dictate the trajectory of sterling and the yield curve. Additionally, the reaction to Nvidia’s decline will be a bellwether for the broader tech sector; if the semiconductor slump is contained, we may see a rotation back into AI and tech growth, but if it spreads, the defensive bias in gold and bonds will likely persist. The coming days will test the resilience of the current risk-on sentiment against the backdrop of tightening monetary policy expectations.
