The defining narrative of this trading week was the Bank of England’s decisive pivot, which sent shockwaves through both equity and fixed-income markets. While the central bank held interest rates steady at 3.75%, the market reaction was dominated not by the rate decision itself, but by an unexpected and significant overhaul of its quantitative tightening programme. This technical shift in how the BoE manages its bond sales has been interpreted by major institutions like Barclays and UBS as a hawkish signal, with both banks now forecasting a potential rate hike as early as November. This shift in sentiment weighed heavily on the FTSE 100, which saw a sharp intraday decline of 453 points to 10,710 before recovering to close the week with a modest 1.25% gain, marking its strongest weekly performance since July. The volatility underscores a growing anxiety among institutional investors that the path to disinflation may be steeper and more prolonged than previously modeled, particularly as inflation risks mount.

In the energy and commodities space, the week was characterized by a tug-of-war between geopolitical fears and fundamental supply data. Brent crude prices spiked to USD 102.50 on Thursday, driven by lingering concerns over potential Saudi disruption, but subsequently retreated as those fears eased, providing some relief to energy-intensive sectors. This volatility directly impacted the performance of UK energy stocks, which weighed on the broader index alongside banking shares. However, the resilience of the FTSE 100 suggests that large-cap energy names are still benefiting from the higher-for-longer oil price environment, even as the market digests the new monetary policy framework. For our clients in the energy sector, the key takeaway is that while spot price volatility remains high, the structural support from elevated oil prices continues to underpin earnings forecasts, albeit with increased sensitivity to geopolitical headlines.

On the corporate front, activity remained robust, with a mix of defensive positioning and opportunistic M&A. In the technology and digital assets space, global sentiment was buoyed by strong moves in US-listed peers, with Generac jumping nearly 30% on a significant Amazon deal and Nebius gaining over 9%, signaling continued appetite for infrastructure and AI-adjacent plays. Domestically, we saw continued engagement from asset managers, with abrdn Property Income Trust reporting interim results that highlighted a narrowing loss per share, suggesting stabilization in the real assets sector. Meanwhile, Foresight Group Holdings executed transactions in its own shares, a move often interpreted as a vote of confidence in the group’s long-term value proposition. In the retail and consumer space, Wizz Air and Softcat drew analyst attention, reflecting the ongoing rotation between growth and value strategies as investors navigate the uncertain macro landscape.

The broader macro picture remains complex, with sterling trading flat against the euro despite a stronger-than-expected UK Retail Sales print, indicating that the currency market is currently prioritizing monetary policy divergence over domestic economic data. The Bank of England’s bond-sale shake-up has also sparked debate regarding its impact on mortgage rates, with warnings from consumer advocates like Martin Lewis highlighting the potential cost to savers and borrowers. Looking ahead, the market is now in a holding pattern, waiting for clarity on how the new BoE framework will translate into actual borrowing costs. For institutional clients, the focus should shift from immediate rate cuts to the sustainability of current valuations in a higher-rate environment. Tomorrow, we will be watching closely for any further commentary from the BoE on the implementation of its new bond sale programme, as well as any late-week positioning by large funds ahead of the weekend, which could set the tone for the next leg of the market’s journey.
