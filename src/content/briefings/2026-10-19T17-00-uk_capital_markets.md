The defining narrative of the trading day was a sharp rotation out of high-flying technology and AI equities, triggered by a broader sell-off in Wall Street that spilled over into London. The FTSE 100 closed lower by 0.9% at 10,779.15, while the FTSE 250 slipped 0.3%, reflecting a cautious mood as investors reassessed valuations in the tech sector. This retreat from growth stocks coincided with a notable flight to quality in fixed income, where UK gilts saw significant buying interest. The 30-year gilt yield hit 5.8%, the highest among G7 nations, yet major asset managers including M&G, Ninety One, and Mediolanum International Funds stepped in to accumulate British government bonds. This divergence suggests that despite concerns over fiscal discipline and the "safe budget" bet, institutional capital is currently prioritizing yield and safety over equity risk, creating a complex backdrop for risk-on assets.

In the technology and semiconductor space, the sentiment was mixed but leaning toward consolidation. While the broader AI trade faced headwinds from US markets, domestic sentiment showed pockets of resilience. IQE, a key player in the semiconductor supply chain, saw increased retail interest, appearing on lists of most-bought stocks in ISAs for the first time, indicating that some investors are still chasing exposure to the chip cycle despite the broader tech pullback. However, the regulatory environment is tightening around the sector. The Financial Conduct Authority has actively called on AI providers to include clear disclaimers when offering financial advice via large language models, signaling a move toward stricter oversight of generative AI applications in consumer finance. This regulatory clarity may dampen speculative enthusiasm for unregulated AI startups but could benefit established, compliant players in the data and analytics space, such as London Stock Exchange Group, which continues to anchor the market with its robust infrastructure business.

The energy and commodities complex provided a counter-narrative to the tech weakness, driven by shifting oil dynamics. With crude prices slipping from the $110 per barrel mark, refining margins for players like Ampol doubled, highlighting the sensitivity of downstream energy profits to crude volatility. This shift underscores the ongoing tension between energy security and inflationary pressures, a theme that is likely to keep energy stocks in focus for income-seeking investors. Meanwhile, geopolitical and regulatory risks extended beyond the UK, with France opening a probe into the creator platform Mym over suspected rule breaches, and Indonesian markets suffering a record two-day rout due to MSCI expulsion warnings. These events highlight a fragmented global risk landscape, where regional regulatory actions and index reclassifications are driving localized volatility that can quickly impact global sentiment.

Looking at the currency markets, the pound remains supported by expectations of a Bank of England rate hike in November, contingent on upcoming jobs and inflation data. The EUR/USD pair is hovering near 1.10, pressured by energy fears, which complicates the outlook for European exporters. For institutional clients, the current environment demands a nuanced approach: while the gilt rally offers attractive yields, the premium for safety is high, and the risk of a fiscal surprise remains embedded in the pricing. The Zilch IPO plans also serve as a litmus test for the valuation of UK BNPL lenders under FCA rules, potentially setting a precedent for fintech valuations in a regulated environment. The market is currently balancing the allure of high-yield bonds against the long-term growth potential of tech and energy, with the latter two sectors showing signs of selective strength despite the broader caution.

Tomorrow, the focus will shift to the UK jobs and inflation data, which will be critical in confirming or challenging the market’s bet on a November BoE hike, potentially driving further volatility in sterling and gilt yields.
