The City of London opened the week in a state of defensive retreat, with the FTSE 100 sliding into the red as global investors reassessed their risk appetite in light of mounting fears regarding further interest rate hikes. The prevailing narrative is one of caution; after a period of relative stability, the market is now pricing in a more hawkish trajectory for central banks, particularly the Federal Reserve. This shift has triggered a broad-based selloff, with equities struggling to find support as yields climb. For institutional clients, the immediate implication is a re-rating of growth assets, particularly in the technology and AI sectors, where high valuations are increasingly vulnerable to rising discount rates. The sentiment is clear: liquidity is tightening, and the era of easy money is firmly behind us, forcing a hard reset in capital allocation strategies.

Sterling has borne the brunt of this global risk-off sentiment, striking a one-month low against the Japanese Yen and facing significant pressure against the US Dollar. The pound’s weakness is not merely a domestic phenomenon but a symptom of a wider global bond selloff that is rocking markets from London to New York. With the Bank of England’s Governor Andrew Bailey facing increased scrutiny over the pace of monetary tightening, the currency remains under pressure. This volatility is already trickling down to the consumer level, with experts urging households to secure foreign currency for travel before further depreciation, while mortgage rates are poised to soar as lenders adjust to the new cost of capital. The first mainstream building society has already announced increased charges for customers, signaling that the transmission mechanism from central bank policy to retail finance is accelerating.

In the corporate arena, the lack of major M&A announcements or specific sector-specific catalysts in defence, semiconductors, or energy suggests that dealmakers are pausing to wait for clarity. The uncertainty surrounding interest rates has created a "wait and see" atmosphere, particularly for capital-intensive sectors like energy and aerospace, where project financing costs are directly linked to bond yields. While there is no single dominant story in the M&A space today, the underlying trend is one of consolidation and caution. Companies are likely to delay acquisitions or refinancing activities until the direction of travel for rates becomes clearer, leading to a quieter period for corporate action but a more volatile one for existing holdings.

Looking at the broader trends, the market is undergoing a fundamental shift from a growth-led rally to a value and income-focused strategy. The surge in bond yields is making fixed-income assets more attractive relative to equities, forcing a rotation out of high-multiple tech stocks and into defensive sectors or cash. This is not a temporary blip but a structural adjustment to a higher-for-longer interest rate environment. For our institutional clients, this means that portfolio rebalancing is no longer optional but essential. The focus must shift from capital appreciation to capital preservation, with a keen eye on companies with strong balance sheets and low debt levels that can withstand a prolonged period of tight monetary conditions.

Tomorrow, the market will be watching closely for any signs of stabilization in the bond markets, as well as any commentary from central bank officials that might offer a glimmer of hope for a pause in rate hikes. The key question is whether the current selloff is a correction or the beginning of a deeper bear market. For now, the defining story is the power of the bond market to dictate equity performance, and investors must adapt their strategies accordingly.
