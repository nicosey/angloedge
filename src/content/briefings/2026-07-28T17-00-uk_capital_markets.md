Today's UK Capital Markets Digest

The defence sector continues to demonstrate its resilience as a core pillar of the UK’s industrial strategy, with Exosens reporting strong first-half performance for 2026. Driven by sustained momentum in night vision technology and accelerating demand for advanced defense imaging solutions, the company has revised its full-year outlook upward. This result underscores the structural tailwinds supporting the UK defence supply chain, where governments are prioritizing domestic capability and technological sovereignty over cost arbitrage. For investors, this signals that niche hardware providers with proven export credentials and government contracts remain in a privileged position to benefit from prolonged geopolitical uncertainty and increased military spending across NATO allies.

In the broader macro landscape, market attention is firmly fixed on the Bank of England’s upcoming interest rate decision, with consensus pricing in a hold at 3.75 percent. The pound has slipped against the euro as easing energy prices in the Eurozone have bolstered the single currency, yet the BoE faces a delicate balancing act. With oil prices recently spiking above $100 a barrel, inflationary pressures remain sticky, complicating the central bank’s path to its 2 percent target. While the market expects no change on Thursday, any hawkish language regarding future rate cuts could provide a much-needed lift for sterling and support equity valuations that have been weighed down by persistent uncertainty over the cost of capital.

On the banking front, valuation disparities are widening among major lenders, with Barclays trading at a significant discount to peers like HSBC on a price-to-book basis. This divergence highlights shifting investor sentiment toward UK financials, where domestic-focused banks face different headwinds compared to their global counterparts. Meanwhile, in the smaller cap space, Aberforth Smaller Companies Trust released its half-year report, reflecting the ongoing challenges and opportunities within the mid-market. The mixed performance across these segments suggests that stock selection is becoming increasingly critical, as broad beta plays are less likely to outperform in a fragmented economic environment where sector-specific drivers outweigh general market trends.

As we wrap up the trading day, the defining narrative remains the tension between geopolitical strength in defence and macroeconomic fragility in rates and currency. Markets closed with a cautious tone, reflecting the wait for BoE clarity and the broader implications of energy price volatility. Tomorrow, investors should watch for any dovish or hawkish surprises from the Bank of England that could trigger sharp moves in gilts and sterling, alongside further developments in global oil markets that may influence inflation expectations heading into the next earnings season.
