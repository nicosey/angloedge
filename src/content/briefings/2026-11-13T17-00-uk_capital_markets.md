The defining story of the week was the sharp acceleration in UK gilt yields, with short-dated government bonds hitting their highest levels in over three years. This move was driven by a sudden surge in market expectations for interest rate hikes, reflecting a broader global shift as investors reassess the trajectory of monetary policy. The spike in yields has created immediate pressure on long-duration assets, particularly in the technology and green energy sectors, where valuations are highly sensitive to discount rates. For institutional clients, this signals a potential rotation out of high-multiple growth stocks and into more defensive or income-generating positions, as the cost of capital rises and the appeal of speculative AI and semiconductor plays is tempered by tighter financial conditions.

In the corporate landscape, the proposed merger involving ProVen Growth and Income VCT plc stands out as a significant development in the venture capital space. With the offer dated for November 2026, this transaction highlights the ongoing consolidation within the UK’s VCT sector as managers seek to achieve scale and improve liquidity in a challenging fundraising environment. While the immediate impact on the broader market is limited, it underscores a trend of rationalization in private markets, where efficiency and capital preservation are becoming paramount. This move may encourage further M&A activity among smaller asset managers looking to streamline operations and reduce overheads in a period of heightened regulatory scrutiny and competitive pressure.

From a sectoral perspective, the narrative around AI and data software remains mixed. While six key data software stocks continue to demonstrate how AI is driving operational efficiency and accelerating growth for some, the sector is not immune to the macroeconomic headwinds. For certain firms, AI has become a source of revenue volatility rather than pure upside, indicating that the market is beginning to distinguish between genuine value creation and hype. This divergence is crucial for investors in the technology and semiconductor spaces, who must now look beyond top-line growth metrics to assess underlying profitability and cash flow resilience. The energy and defence sectors, meanwhile, remain relatively insulated from the rate shock, offering a stable counterweight to the volatility seen in tech-heavy portfolios.

Looking ahead, the focus must shift to the US economic calendar, which remains the primary driver of global liquidity and currency movements. As the US dollar strengthens in response to hawkish signals, sterling may face further depreciation, impacting the earnings of UK multinationals with significant overseas revenue. The interplay between UK monetary policy and US data releases will be critical in determining the next phase of gilt yields and equity valuations. Investors should monitor the divergence between UK and US rate expectations, as this gap could create opportunities for cross-border arbitrage or sector-specific rotations.

Tomorrow, watch the reaction of the FTSE 100 to the latest US inflation data, as any further confirmation of sticky prices could trigger a broader sell-off in growth stocks and reinforce the flight to quality in UK government bonds.
