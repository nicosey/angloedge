The defining narrative for today’s session was a decisive reversal of the recent tech-led volatility, with the FTSE 100 stabilizing and global indices rebounding sharply after the US market entered correction territory last week. This recovery was not driven by a sudden surge in growth optimism, but rather by a tactical rotation out of overvalued technology names and into defensive, value-oriented sectors. UK shares climbed from a five-month low, buoyed by strong performance in banks and mining stocks, which provided crucial support as investors digested data indicating a slight slowdown in the UK’s private sector growth. The market is clearly attempting to decouple from the US tech narrative, finding its own footing in domestic fundamentals and cyclical recovery, even as the broader global sentiment remains fragile.

Underlying this price action is a growing anxiety among fund managers who fear we are on the brink of a broader stock market meltdown, prompting a shift in portfolio construction toward protection against an AI bubble burst. While Anthropic’s recent debut saw shares soar nearly 40 percent in the first minutes, signaling continued hype in the generative AI space, the broader reaction has been one of caution. Leveraged funds are facing margin calls and redemption pressures, forcing them to sell the "easiest to sell and most profitable" assets to raise cash. This dynamic suggests that the current rebound may be driven more by forced selling and liquidity management than by genuine fundamental improvement, creating a volatile environment where sentiment can swing rapidly on any new macro data.

In the corporate and regulatory landscape, activity remains robust despite the market uncertainty. Private equity giants Permira and Ares are reportedly in the running for the £8bn national advice group Finli, which is currently backed by JC Flowers. This signals that PE appetite for financial services and advice platforms remains strong, even as valuations adjust. Simultaneously, the regulatory environment for digital assets is tightening, with the FCA finalizing rules that bar UK crypto exchanges from acting as market makers on their own platforms, a move set to take effect in 2027. This regulatory clarity, alongside iFOREX’s in-principle approval from the UAE, highlights a bifurcation in the crypto sector, with some players seeking offshore flexibility while UK-based entities face stricter operational constraints.

From a macro perspective, the pound-dollar pair is under pressure, with forecasts suggesting a slide to fresh lows if core PCE inflation data reinforces hawkish bets from the Federal Reserve. This divergence between resilient UK PMIs and a potentially tighter US monetary policy creates a complex backdrop for sterling, which may struggle to find support despite domestic economic stability. The interplay between global rate expectations and local growth data will be the key determinant for currency flows in the coming weeks.

Tomorrow, the focus will shift to whether this rebound can sustain itself without further catalysts, particularly as we await more detailed insights into the impact of the FCA’s new crypto regulations on market liquidity and the next round of US inflation data that could further sway Fed expectations.
