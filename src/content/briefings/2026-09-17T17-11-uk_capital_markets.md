Today’s UK capital markets session was defined by a decisive divergence in global monetary policy, as the Bank of England held its base rate at 3.75% while the Federal Reserve hiked by 25 basis points. This move, the sixth consecutive hold by the MPC, signals a cautious stance from Governor Andrew Bailey, who cited an outlook too uncertain to justify further tightening despite UK inflation rising to 3.1%. The decision has immediate implications for the housing sector, where lenders including Nationwide and Santander have already pre-emptively raised mortgage rates, suggesting that the cost of borrowing remains a persistent headwind for consumer credit and property sales, which continue to show year-on-year declines.

The most significant market reaction, however, came from the fixed income desk, where UK government bonds rallied sharply after the Bank of England announced it would pause its quantitative tightening programme by scrapping plans to sell long-dated gilts. This strategic shift in bond management was welcomed by investors as a stabilizing measure, helping to cap yields and support equity valuations. The FTSE 100 responded positively, climbing on the back of this bond rally and a stronger dollar, with retail, mining, and industrial stocks leading the gains. This divergence from the ECB and Fed highlights the unique position of the UK economy, balancing the need to control inflation against the risk of stifling growth in a fragile economic environment.

In the technology and digital asset space, the regulatory landscape tightened considerably as the Financial Conduct Authority issued final guidance on crypto authorization rules, signaling that mere registration will no longer suffice for market participants. The FCA, alongside HMRC and police, also targeted three London-based peer-to-peer crypto trading sites in a crackdown on illegal activity, underscoring a broader trend of increased enforcement. While this regulatory clarity may provide a foundation for future growth, it also highlights the UK’s lagging position in crypto adoption compared to peers like Germany, where family offices and wealth managers are increasingly integrating digital assets. For institutional clients, this suggests a period of consolidation and compliance-heavy operations for fintech and crypto firms, with applications for the new regime opening in late September.

From a sector-specific perspective, the defence and energy narratives remain critical. A new leadership appointment at a major Plymouth-based company underscores the strategic importance of the defence sector in an era of global tension, with priorities clearly focused on adapting to the rapidly changing nature of warfare. Meanwhile, energy prices are forecast to jump by 25% in the coming winter, a development that could exacerbate inflationary pressures and complicate the Bank of England’s path. This combination of rising energy costs and a pause in gilt sales creates a complex backdrop for investors, where the focus shifts from pure rate sensitivity to resilience against external shocks.

Looking ahead, the market’s attention will turn to the upcoming Budget, where Chancellor Rachel Reeves will need to navigate the tension between fiscal consolidation and the Bank of England’s inflation warning that prices may top 4% early next year. The key question for tomorrow is whether the pause in gilt sales will be sufficient to stabilize bond markets or if further intervention will be required as energy prices bite into household budgets.
